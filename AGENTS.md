# Stop the Invasion

## Active product agreement — Kestrel expedition

The active game is Kestrel: an illustrated space expedition with a branching sector chart, ship-card combat and crew away missions. The entry is `src/main.ts` → `src/ship/shell.ts`, with controlled ship combat in `src/ship/ui.ts` and one renderer in `src/ship/scene.ts`. This supersedes the MOREMART presentation and temporal gameplay for the active application.

The expedition connects twelve authored nodes, a one-enemy introduction, duplicate-role encounters and a three-ship blockade. Hull persists; salvage, exact-card refits, finite services, science choices and four collectible crew support the journey. Select up to three crew for diplomacy or deterministic away combat. Reaching the Far Relay is not victory until the player explicitly transmits the survey. Ship defeat and expedition completion have distinct outcomes.

`docs/design/kestrel-expedition.md` is the implementation contract for GitHub goal #12 and supersedes the former combat-only limit. Main owns `src/ship/expedition-types.ts`, `expedition-content.ts` and `expedition.ts`. Optional modules, imbuements and emergency-supply inventories in `docs/design/kestrel-ship-systems.md` remain design proposals, not implemented mechanics or balanced content. No staffing simulator, permanent statistical unlocks or time manipulation is introduced.

The simulation has player/victory/defeat states. Enemy turns resolve atomically into ordered presentation events. `dispatchBattle` clones before commitment; malformed commands, unexpected own keys, bad phases, missing/invalid targets and unaffordable plays are atomic failures. Mutable enemy recipe graphs are independent between battles. Reuse the deterministic helper in `src/game/random.ts`; never inspect hidden draw order when choosing actions.

Kestrel starts at 70 hull, 12 shield, recharge 3, five cards and three energy. `SHIP_RULES` owns these numbers. Ordinary plays pay first, resolve immediately and go to discard; they do not receive an automatic replacement. Reserve Cell grants two energy and exhausts for this fight; current energy can exceed the three-energy turn refill. Tactical Sweep explicitly draws two. Adaptive Coils adds two to the first shield card each player turn, within shield capacity; its ready/spent state and effective card preview agree.

End Turn discards the unplayed hand. Living enemies recharge their own shields, then execute one authored intent in stable order. Damage depletes shield before hull; multi-hits are separate and lethal suppresses remaining hits/actions. On survival, increment the turn, reset energy to three, recharge Kestrel, reset Coils and draw five. No post-defeat refill, draw or later enemy action. Discard recycles only when draw is empty; emit one anonymous recycle event with a count, never shuffled card identities or order.

Six base definitions—Pulse Cannon, Shield Cycle, Rail Lance, Reserve Cell, Tactical Sweep and Twin Burst—are joined by four unique crew cards for Captain Vale, Iona Voss, Rex Calder and Dr. Sen. The persistent starter deck replaces one Pulse copy with Vale. Grades 0–2 use authored effect scaling; a crew card's grade stays synchronized with that crew member's level 1–3. Role recipes for Corsair, Needle and Bulwark provide seven intents across the authored encounter rosters. Instances retain distinct IDs, including duplicate roles. Multiple living targets require an explicit choice; a sole legal target is automatic. Unaffordable hand cards remain inspectable.

Card frame/header color means rarity: Common steel/ivory (`#c9cfca`), Uncommon blue (`#326b9f`), Rare gold (`#d9b45e`). Base identities remain Pulse = Common/Weapons, Shield = Common/Engineering, Burst = Uncommon/Weapons, Cell = Uncommon/Engineering, Sweep = Uncommon/Command and Lance = Rare/Weapons. Crew cards are Uncommon, with Command/Engineering/Weapons/Science identities. Monochrome footer emblems and printed labels identify departments. Attack/System/Crew remains mechanical kind. Rarity grants no bonus and does not change draw odds or costs.

Native hand labels, selection, details and visible pile entries repeat the classification without relying on color; the draw pile remains anonymous. Research drafts have no approved rarity and display Unrated, with department derived from their authored kind until explicitly classified. Research title/flavor rendering fits to a bounded minimum size then ellipsizes rather than escaping the card; native inspection retains full text. Classification texture identity includes both card ID and mechanical kind.

## Temporal contracts

Use **Stretch** and **Compress**, not Haste/Slow or fractional player time. Normal enemies take one basic action per player tick. Stretch requires two ticks per enemy basic action. Compress permits two ordered enemy basic actions per player tick, including pursuit followed by an attack when legal. The player always spends whole ticks. Reapplying the same mode refreshes duration without erasing partial progress; changing mode resets enemy progress. Expiry is handled by the canonical tick resolver.

Scouting extends the number of future ticks shown for a limited duration. It does not reveal future draw order, grant extra actions, create reachable targets through walls or change the underlying clock.

Paid retention keeps the chosen exact hand card after its next play instead of replacing it, then consumes the retention mark. Innate Retain remains an authored card trait. Borrow draws its authored extra cards, exhausts its source and skips the next replacement opportunities, including the source's ordinary replacement. Explicit extra draws do not repay that debt; only one outstanding Borrow is allowed. There is no encounter-boundary hand or exhaust reset.

Echo selects an eligible recorded ordinary combat occurrence by `sourceId`, separately from the present `targetId`. It replays the recorded base recipe and signed grade against current defenses. It does not copy old HP deltas, source payments, draw/energy/time utilities or descendant Echoes. A source occurrence may be used once in the current branch. Defense-only Echo filters the whole recipe.

Rewind is actual world restoration, not editing a displayed card or replaying a fixed later command list. It is available in playing or defeat when a legal prior snapshot exists within eight ticks and a charge remains. Restore positions, HP/statuses, intentions/progress, inventory zones, grades, retention, Echo-use state, random state, temporal modes and progression flags; truncate the abandoned history/checkpoints. A different subsequent decision creates a different branch.

Rewind spends one nonrestored charge. Rewind source-card exhaustion provenance also survives rollback and removes those exact UIDs from restored zones, preventing duplicate sources or self-refunding loops. Reward/service checkpoints refresh the charge and establish a new earliest snapshot. `Valid Receipt` now invokes Rewind; the former exact-card Reclaim and move/swap Rewrite APIs are not active world mechanics.
