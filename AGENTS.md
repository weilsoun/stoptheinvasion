# Stop the Invasion

## Product agreement
Single-player party deckbuilder, initially Bob versus infected store security in a replayable 1v1 combat laboratory. Future party of three and later two-player co-op are not this milestone. Campaign, map/shop/boss, items (potions), collectible statuses (relics), art generation, environmental rules, and menus follow combat validation.

1920x1080 design coordinates; locked 16:9 with letterboxing. Bob LEFT facing right; enemies RIGHT facing left. Comic-book horror comedy in MOREMART, a parody big-box store. Physical lit PlayCanvas cards with rounded corners, readable hover, animated pickup and action emphasis. Hand is fanned; queue is a straight, pronounced staggered stack. Mouse, keyboard and touch; no physics-driven combat.

## Rules
Six fixed internal timing positions resolve RIGHT TO LEFT: index5 first, then4..0, then cleanup. Enemy intents stay anchored and exact; empty positions advance without firing. Cards to the right of an enemy act before it; cards to its left act afterward. Tiny dots indicate empty positions; no visible numbers, placement arrows or ghost outlines. Pickup eases the card's center to the cursor and follows freely; release near the nearest non-enemy point places it instantly. Resting queue positions, scale and all depth ordering update synchronously, never spring or bounce during reordering. Insertion previews remain pure and keep the chosen point visible. Inserting into an occupied player position shifts toward the nearest empty non-enemy position; distance ties favor the right. Occupied queued moves rotate intervening player cards while preserving gaps and enemy anchors. Invalid/full/outside drops never mutate state.

Ordinary cards automatically choose their sole valid living target when placed: damage attacks the only enemy, self-effects apply to Bob. No target dots/strips or actor-selection highlights for obvious choices. More than one valid choice requires an explicit target; no living target rejects placement without mutation. Attachments require a card or position and never show actor selectors. Unaffordable hand cards use opaque grayscale faces and cannot start play/targeting through mouse, keyboard or touch, but remain inspectable. Affordability uses current energy minus all commitments; zero-cost cards stay playable at zero. Refunds immediately re-enable affordable cards. Native attachment-refund controls remain usable inside disabled cards, and an unaffordable card may receive an affordable attachment. Energy bubbles distinguish available, reserved and empty capacity; generated energy cannot finance current commitments. Hands-off resolution; immediate lethal stops battle and dead actors never act. Block expires at resolution end. Exposed adds a flat bonus to the next incoming hit, then is consumed; stacks add. Healing caps at maximum HP without changing Block/Exposed. Draw five, start energy two, gain two subsequently, cap four; values live on actors. Unplayed cards discard unless retained; resolution-drawn cards survive into next planning. No full outcome preview. Seeded reshuffle and replay are deterministic.

Attachment cards take NO timeline position. Drop onto a compatible attack card or an empty timing point; an occupied card's exposed lower hit area targets its fixed position. Card-body targets bind exact UID and follow between hand/queue; position targets stay put, including empty positions. Only compatible living-owner damaging cards receive card attachments; position attachments affect only compatible occupants. Attachments reserve energy, can be removed/refunded before Resolve, and spend once at resolution start. Signed damage changes stack and floor each damage effect at zero before Block/Exposed. All attachments expire into discard at turn end, including terminal cleanup, empty positions and unplayed hand targets. Unused attachments still cost energy. Base card/intent effects never mutate.

## Architecture and ownership
- `src/game/types.ts`: shared types, Main owns contract changes.
- `src/game/content.ts`: data and encounter tuning, Main owns during parallel build.
- `src/game/combat.ts`: combat agent owns deterministic rules, zero DOM/PlayCanvas imports.
- `src/view/types.ts`: Main owns scene/UI interface.
- `src/view/scene.ts`: scene agent owns PlayCanvas renderer and procedural original artwork.
- `src/ui.ts`, `src/style.css`: interaction agent owns HUD, hand hit targets, drag targeting, queue editing, resolution animation orchestration.
- `src/main.ts`, `index.html`, package/config files: Main owns integration.

Combat API: `createCombat(seed?: number): CombatState`; `availableEnergy(state, actor?: ActorId): number`; `queueCard(state, uid, target: ActorId | null, slot: number): CommandResult`; `previewPlacement(state, uid, target, slot): QueueSlot[] | null`; `removeCard(state, slot)`; `moveCard(state, from, to)`; `retargetCard(state, slot, target)`; `resolveTurn(state): ResolutionStep[]`. Attachment API: `canAttachModifier(state, uid, target): boolean`; `attachModifier(state, uid, target)`; `removeModifier(state, uid)`; `damageModifier(state, uid, slot: number | null): number`. Mutating planning commands return CommandResult and mutate only on success. ModifierTarget is `{kind:'card', uid}` or `{kind:'slot', slot}`; attachments hold their source card and target. Preview is pure, shallow and accepts a hand or queued UID, preserving queued targets. Explicit position is required; modifiers cannot enter the ordinary queue. Reject invalid/dead explicit action targets, invalid/noninteger indices, wrong modifier targets and commands outside planning. Resolve preflights all commitments before spending or mutation, returns independent snapshots for six positions plus cleanup, and does not mutate its input. Attachment cards/target objects are cloned too. Generated resources cannot finance commitments. Logs are bounded; Block, discard/retention, energy/draw refresh and next-turn intent installation remain deterministic.
Draw/discard CombatEvents carry independent `cards` snapshots. Cleanup emits discard before draw, even when the same UID is discarded and immediately redrawn by reshuffling. Presentation must consume these events, not infer movements from final-pile membership.

Renderer API: `createScene(canvas): ScenePort`; contract in view/types.ts. CardVisual uses top-left design x/y, dimensions and rotation. Physical cards/text/dots are meshes, not DOM replicas. Fields include target/targets, dragged, locked, damageModifier, underCard, detail, flip and snap. `getCardPose(uid)` returns actual displayed bounds/rotation/flip. Resting queued cards snap x/y, dimensions and roll synchronously in setCards; spring velocities reset. Depth is always immediate. Hand pickup and airborne actions retain position springs and eased dimensions. Reduced motion snaps all poses. Hand fans at2.3 degrees per offset; hover and held cards straighten. Queue cards stay straight, shrink2% and rise7px per card in front; rightmost is on top. Queue nominal172x241, y350, stride132, first left544. Firing cards enlarge to135% and move to their actual target. Player cards then flip into the discard pile; enemy intents exit off-right and never enter Bob's inventory. Generated comic raster illustrations are required and preloaded without a geometric fallback. Enemy frames invert player light/dark panels while preserving artwork colors. Alpha-aware512x768 textures have rounded corners. DPR cap1.5; actor centers Bob(410,400), guard(1500,400). Hand hover280x392 at y616 ends1008. UI API: `mountGame(root, scene): GamePort`; fixed1920x1080 root scaled by Main. Same UID survives hand, queue and flight. Pointer-held cards animate to cursor center; release near a non-enemy timing point snaps the queue immediately. Dragging a queued card back into the hand returns it and refunds its cost while preserving bound attachments; Delete/Backspace provides keyboard removal. No floating Remove button. Queued attachments sit behind hosts with grabbable headers; pulling one off refunds it, canceling preserves it. Card clicks open centered details over a dimmed board, including unaffordable cards. Close, Escape and outside-card clicks/taps dismiss and restore focus; card-interior clicks do not dismiss. Detail controls preserve keyboard/touch play, move and refund actions. During resolution, consume descending snapshots: lift150ms, travel360ms to actual target, adopt effects at impact, hold130ms, then discard the player card or eject the enemy before the next action. Animate draws from the draw pile and discards into the opposite pile with flips. A recycled UID visibly discards first, snaps to draw origin, then deals back. presentedQueue excludes airborne/completed UIDs without mutating combat snapshots. Cleanup clears exclusions. Outcomes wait until playback completes. Every wait checks sequence/destroy guards; restart and teardown cancel timers and resolve pending waits. Compact Turn/Menu controls replace the title/restart banner; Escape opens the menu when no interaction or modal needs canceling.

## Required balance gate
Every new card requires a fresh balance run against the current rules. Rerun after changes to costs, effects, deck composition, enemy schedules, resources or timing rules; art-only changes do not require numerical rebalancing. `npm run balance` uses32 paired seeds per candidate/policy, writes `.balance/report.json`, and prints policy outcomes, opportunity-normalized card usage, role gaps, tested tuning candidates and uncertainty. CLI supports `--seeds N --output PATH`. The fingerprint includes game data/rules and analysis source; old results do not validate changed rules. The workflow `.github/workflows/balance.yml` runs on game/analysis changes and uploads the report, including coverage failures. Player catalog cards without any exercised play/attachment fail coverage; new enemy intents also need explicit behavioral verification.
Suggestions must cite measured before/after candidates, not infer power from raw popularity. Strong, imperfect and greedy policies are heuristics, not human or optimal-play proofs. Preserve strong-play viability while making mistakes matter; never optimize solely for aggregate win rate. Distinguish exploratory low-use playtest leads from supported balance recommendations. No automatic production tuning. When timing/shape rules change, update the planner and metrics and rerun; current six-slot results do not validate a shaped-card system.
